## Scientific and privacy rules

1. **Never claim clinical validity, diagnostic accuracy, unique-lesion sensitivity, independent patient generalization, physician review-time benefit, novelty or publication readiness without corresponding evidence.** `blood=1` is a frame annotation, not a lesion ID; `blood=0` is not proof of a normal examination.
2. **Preserve examination separation.** Original training exams 1–8 and new 21/23/25/27/29/30 have been used for exploratory development; historical 9–10 were inspected. **Future Galar evaluation 11–20 and the original authors' reserved 61–80 remain unopened.** Do not inspect their labels/pixels, use them to rank acquisitions or optimize models, or redefine them as fitting data.
3. **Do not tune to previously examined transfer outcomes and call the next comparison independent.** Before further model fitting, freeze model configurations, seeds, source IDs, train/held-out exam assignments, image selection policy, metrics and model-selection decision. For correlations/uncertainty, examine examination-level observations; do not treat correlated frames or short annotation runs as independent patients/lesions.
4. **Do not commit or publish** raw research images, labels, study-identifying source frame manifests, individual predictions, trained weights, SQLite workspaces, connected-app private data or Xona confidential information. Model outputs, source annotations and human notes must remain separate in UI and exports.
5. Reviewer-facing UI must not silently hide frames, invent original timing, interpolate missing annotations, or automatically turn AI predictions into clinical conclusions. A 10% review budget was an experimental point, **not** an imposed product restriction. Source-index spacing is not a verified video frame rate or event duration.
6. Keep the repository small, clear, auditable and professional; preserve prior measured failures and do not overwrite protocols/metrics. Do not invent a software licence or make a public announcement without owner authorization. Prefer reusing proven code and only the tests that exercise real behavior.
