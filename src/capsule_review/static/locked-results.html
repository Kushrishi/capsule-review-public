Where AI image ranking did—and did not—preserve evidence.

Explore the public, non-identifying aggregates from the locked ten-examination Galar 11–20 evaluation. Compare equal-workload policies using distinct blood-positive annotation runs, not merely the count of positive images found.

Primary finding: At 10% workload, the score-bin policy did not outperform uniform sampling: 49.35% versus 50.62% mean positive-run recall (−1.27 percentage points across six defined examinations). The +9.51-point result at 25% was a prespecified secondary observation, influenced heavily by examination 15. This study is not clinical validation.

Run coverage is not positive-image retrieval

Every method reviews the same number of source positions at the selected workload. A method may hit many positive frames from one run while missing other runs entirely. These labels are dataset annotations, not separately adjudicated lesions.

Interpretation and methodological limits

This view contains only previously published aggregate values and does not load medical images, source labels, individual prediction scores or private research packages. Positive annotation runs are consecutive labeled regions at the dataset's original +5 source-index stride; they are not proven unique clinical lesions. The six positive-run examinations are not verified as patient-independent. Skipped-position spans are ordinal positions, not clinical time or safety. Galar 11–20 was evaluated and cannot be reused as an untouched test set.

The original model, policies, endpoints and 10% primary result remain unchanged. No model was refitted or promoted, and the authors' reserved Galar 61–80 outcomes remain unopened. The private source-image archive is not durably retained. Independent-patient generalization, clinical sensitivity and measured reading-time benefit remain unverified.
